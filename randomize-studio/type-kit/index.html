The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog

A pangram earns its keep: every letter of the alphabet, once, which is the shortest honest way to look at a typeface before you commit a page to it.

Set in Source Serif 4 and Inter · 12 minutes

Type is the one part of a page that has to be read rather than looked at, which makes it the hardest thing to judge from a specimen sheet. A face that is handsome at 96 pixels can close up and turn to mud at 16, and a face that reads beautifully at 16 can look like nothing at all across a title.

So the useful question is never "is this a good typeface" but "is this a good typeface here, at this size, on this measure, against the thing next to it". That is a question you can only answer by trying it on the page itself, which is what the panel is for: pick a block, roll the dice or set it by hand, and read the result where it will actually live.

Hierarchy does most of the work. Size, weight and space between lines will separate a title from a deck from a paragraph long before the choice of face does, and a page whose hierarchy is right will survive almost any face you put it in.

Set it, read it, and change it where it is going to be read.
